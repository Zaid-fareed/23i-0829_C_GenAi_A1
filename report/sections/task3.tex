\section{Task 3: Jointly Trained Soft Mixture of Experts}\label{sec:t3}
\textbf{Model and loss.} A gate $G$ with the classifier's architecture gives $w=\mathrm{softmax}(G(\tilde{x})/\tau)$ and
\begin{equation}
\hat{x}=w_0\tilde{x}+w_1A_{salt}(\tilde{x})+w_2A_{blur}(\tilde{x})+w_3A_{occ}(\tilde{x}),
\end{equation}
where branch~0 is the identity (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch23}, bottom). Gate and experts are trained jointly through the reconstruction error, starting from the trained Task~2 classifier and the final specialists. A warm-up of 2 epochs trains only the gate with the experts frozen; 30 epochs of joint fine-tuning with a much smaller learning rate follow. The loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{MoE}=\lambda_1\mathcal{L}_1+\lambda_s(1-\mathrm{SSIM})+\lambda_c\mathcal{L}_{CE}+\lambda_b\sum_{k=0}^{3}(\bar{w}_k-\tfrac14)^2,
\end{equation}
with $\bar{w}_k$ the mean weight of branch $k$ in a batch and the cross-entropy applied to the raw gate logits. We tie $\lambda_1=\alpha$, $\lambda_s=1-\alpha$, so the five tuned quantities (joint learning rate, temperature, $\lambda_c$, $\lambda_b$, $\alpha$) match the assignment's list. Optuna selected $\tau=2.68$, $\lambda_c=0.48$, $\lambda_b=1.20$, $\alpha=0.61$ and learning rate $4.3\times10^{-5}$; median pruning removed half of the trials. The first mixture inherited the colour loss of its experts and was retrained from the corrected specialists (Section~\ref{sec:t2}).

\textbf{Reconstruction.} The mixture reaches an overall SSIM of \nSoftSSIM{}, against \nHardPredSSIM{} for hard routing and \nTOneSSIMall{} for the universal model (Table~\ref{tab:task3}, Fig.~\ref{fig:bars}). Clean images stay almost untouched (\nSoftSSIMclean{}) because the gate puts most of its weight on the identity branch. On corrupted types it matches the other systems (noise \nSoftSSIMsaltpepper{}, blur \nSoftSSIMblur{}, occlusion \nSoftSSIMocclusion{}); it is slightly better than hard routing at low severity (blur $k=3$: 0.820 against 0.792) and slightly worse at high severity (noise $p=0.15$: 0.778 against 0.790), as a less committed decision would be. Blending does not remove the limit on heavy occlusion.

\textbf{Gate behaviour.} Figure~\ref{fig:heat} gives the mean weight of each branch for every true corruption and severity. The matching expert receives on average \nHomeWeightSalt{} of the weight for salt-and-pepper, \nHomeWeightBlur{} for blur and \nHomeWeightOcc{} for occlusion, and the identity branch \nHomeWeightClean{} for clean images; the weight grows with severity (occlusion: \nGateOccLow{} at 10\% to \nGateOccHigh{} at 35\% coverage). Weights are spread rather than one-hot because of the temperature (mean largest weight \nGateMaxMean{}). Figure~\ref{fig:soft} contrasts inputs on which one expert dominates with inputs on which the weights are distributed; the latter are low-severity corruptions that resemble clean images (mean weight entropy \nEntOccLow{} nats for 10\% occlusions and \nEntClean{} for clean images, against \nEntOccHigh{} for 35\% occlusions). No expert is inactive and none dominates unrelated inputs: each branch is the top branch for a substantial share of inputs (identity \nTopShareclean{}\%, salt-and-pepper \nTopSharesaltpepper{}\%, blur \nTopShareblur{}\%, occlusion \nTopShareocclusion{}\%).

\textbf{Failure cases.} The four worst outputs (SSIM 0.24--0.31, Fig.~\ref{fig:fail3}) are again 35\% occlusions. In the first, a black mask merges with the cat's black background and the mixture produces a grey-brown cloud; in the other three (beagle, basset hound, dark terrier on grass) the gate correctly gives the occlusion expert nearly all the weight, but the expert cannot recreate the animal. They are 0.03--0.04 SSIM below the worst universal cases; because the information is absent, no routing or blending strategy recovers it.
