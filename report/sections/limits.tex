\section{Limitations and Deviations}\label{sec:limits}
We list what a reader should not over-interpret and where our procedure deviates from an ideal one.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The universal model damages inputs that need no restoration.} The bottleneck cannot reproduce fine detail, so clean and mildly blurred images score below the untouched input (Table~\ref{tab:task1}); the routing systems exist to avoid this.
\item \textbf{Specialisation did not improve the corrupted cases.} With equal capacity and bottleneck, the specialists are slightly worse than the universal autoencoder on their own corruption (by 0.01--0.02 SSIM, Table~\ref{tab:routing}); the measured benefit of routing is the identity bypass.
\item \textbf{SSIM rewards leaving small occlusions untouched.} For 10\% occlusions the untouched input has a higher SSIM than any restored output on most inputs, although its PSNR is about 8\,dB lower and the hole is plainly visible; conclusions about occlusion therefore rest on PSNR and visual inspection as well as SSIM. A larger latent, limited skip connections (allowed by the assignment if justified) or a different specialist architecture were not explored.
\item \textbf{Heavy occlusion is irreversible.} No model can recover content that is absent; outputs in masked regions are plausible blur, not the original.
\item \textbf{Small searches.} Each Optuna study has 8--12 trials on short, sub-sampled runs (Table~\ref{tab:optuna}); three Task~4 trials completed. The selected settings are good, not optimal, and the Task~4 search space did not include alternative GAN objectives.
\item \textbf{Two corrected runs.} The first universal model and the first specialists were discarded for the reasons given in Sections~\ref{sec:t1} and~\ref{sec:colour}; their numbers are reported so that the corrections can be checked. The $\alpha$ range of the final specialist and mixture searches was restricted after looking at the outputs.
\item \textbf{Mini-batch balance in Task~3.} Each item of a Task~3 training batch draws its corruption class uniformly, so batches are balanced in expectation but not exactly; the exactly balanced sampler was used for the classifier.
\item \textbf{Evaluation protocol history.} The Kaggle evaluation scripts used a single-condition test manifest (3,669 inputs). The full protocol (36,690 inputs) was evaluated afterwards with the deployed ONNX models rather than the PyTorch checkpoints, because the training checkpoints of the final specialists and mixture were not archived from the cloud session; the ONNX models were verified against PyTorch at export time. The training history of the final mixture is transcribed from the console log.
\item \textbf{Single seed, no confidence intervals.} Results come from one training run per configuration; we report standard deviations over test inputs, not over training seeds.
\item \textbf{Domain.} The restoration models were trained on cats and dogs only; photographs of other subjects (for example faces) are outside their domain and are restored poorly. The cGAN has seen only frontal, near-square FS2K photographs: tall or wide photographs are best cropped, and small faces in wide scenes degrade the sketch.
\item \textbf{Soft sketches and small style 3.} The generated sketches carry about \nSharpShareAll\% of the edge strength of the real ones, and only 46 test pairs belong to style~3.
\item \textbf{PSNR clipping and infinite PSNR.} PSNR is clipped at 60\,dB, so averages that include identity-routed images are not directly comparable with literature values.
\item \textbf{Deployment scope.} Inference runs on the CPU, which is sufficient (tens of milliseconds per image) but was not load-tested; the webcam path needs browser permission and was tested manually only; the second-round Stitch export of the sketch screen was empty.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
A single bottleneck autoencoder restores salt-and-pepper noise well (SSIM \nInputSSIMsaltpepper{} $\rightarrow$ \nTOneSSIMsaltpepper{}) and fills occlusions plausibly (PSNR \nInPSNRocclusion{} $\rightarrow$ \nTOnePSNRocclusion{}\,dB), but it damages images that do not need restoration, such as clean and mildly blurred ones. A classifier with an identity bypass removes that damage (clean SSIM \nTOneSSIMclean{} $\rightarrow$ \nHardSSIMclean{}); the soft, jointly trained mixture does the same (\nSoftSSIMclean{}) without any expert becoming inactive. On corrupted inputs the three systems are within about 0.02 SSIM of one another, so the overall improvement is small (\nTOneSSIMall{} $\rightarrow$ \nHardPredSSIM{} $\rightarrow$ \nSoftSSIM{}) and comes from clean inputs; specialisation itself did not help. The diagnosis of two of our own failures --- an under-fitting first model, and specialists that discarded colour because the loss over-weighted SSIM --- and the disagreement of SSIM and PSNR on occlusion show that aggregate metrics and Optuna objectives are not sufficient: the outputs must be inspected. The style-conditioned cGAN produces recognisable, style-dependent but soft sketches and leaves room for stronger adversarial or perceptual training. The complete system is deployed as a containerised web application with verified ONNX models, tracked experiments and a one-command start-up.
