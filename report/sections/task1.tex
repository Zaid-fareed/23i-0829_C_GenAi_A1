\section{Task 1: Universal Denoising Autoencoder}\label{sec:t1}
\textbf{Architecture.} The encoder halves the spatial size four times ($128\to8$) with widths $48,96,192,384$ (stride-2 and $3\times3$ convolutions, batch norm, ReLU); a $1\times1$ convolution gives a \emph{linear} latent of $32\times8\times8=2{,}048$ values (4.2\% of the 49,152 pixel values) followed by Dropout2d. The decoder mirrors the encoder with $2\times$ nearest-neighbour upsampling and ends with a sigmoid. There are \textbf{no skip connections} and no corruption label is given; the model has 4.01\,M parameters (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch1}).

\textbf{Loss and training.} $\mathcal{L}_{UDAE}=\alpha\,\mathcal{L}_1(x,\hat{x})+(1-\alpha)\bigl(1-\mathrm{SSIM}(x,\hat{x})\bigr)$. Optuna selected $\alpha=0.58$, learning rate $4.6\times10^{-4}$ and batch 16 (Section~\ref{sec:optuna}). Training uses AdamW, cosine decay, gradient clipping, mixed precision on a T4 and 100 epochs over all 2,944 images with fresh random corruptions; the checkpoint with the lowest validation objective $0.5\mathcal{L}_1+0.5(1-\mathrm{SSIM})$ (epoch \nTOneBestEpoch{}) is kept.

\textbf{A first attempt that under-fitted.} Our first run (three-epoch trials, 30 epochs on 736 images) was noticeably fast and weak: SSIM \nAttemptOneSSIM{}, PSNR \nAttemptOnePSNR{}\,dB. The train--validation gap was only 0.008, so the model was under-fitting, not memorising, and clean images scored no better than corrupted ones. Optuna had picked dropout 0.29, which deletes features an autoencoder needs, because very short trials reward fast early learning. We capped dropout at 0.15, lengthened the trials and trained 100 epochs on all data; SSIM on the same manifest rose from \nAttemptOneSSIM{} to \nTOneSSIMsingle{}.

\textbf{Results.} Table~\ref{tab:task1} separates the results by corruption and severity next to the untouched input.
\emph{Salt-and-pepper noise is restored well:} SSIM \nInputSSIMsaltpepper{} $\to$ \nTOneSSIMsaltpepper{}, PSNR \nInPSNRsaltpepper{} $\to$ \nTOnePSNRsaltpepper{}\,dB, almost independent of severity. \emph{Blur is not helped and clean images are damaged:} blur output scores \nTOneSSIMblur{} SSIM against \nInputSSIMblur{} for the input (they tie at $k=5$), and clean images fall from 1.000 to \nTOneSSIMclean{}, because the bottleneck cannot reproduce fine detail; this motivates Tasks~2 and~3. \emph{Occlusion: SSIM and PSNR disagree.} SSIM is \nTOneSSIMocclusion{} against \nInputSSIMocclusion{} for the masked input, yet PSNR rises from \nInPSNRocclusion{} to \nTOnePSNRocclusion{}\,dB. SSIM is windowed, so the large unmasked part keeps the input's score high (doing nothing wins in SSIM on \nOccNoneBetterLow\% of the 10\% masks), while PSNR and Fig.~\ref{fig:ex1} show that the holes are filled with plausible content; only at 35\% coverage does restoration also win in SSIM. \emph{Generalisation:} SSIM on 736 training inputs is \nTrainSSIMsub{} against \nValSSIMsub{} on unseen validation images (gap \nGapSSIM{}); the curves (Fig.~\ref{fig:curves1}) move together.

\textbf{Failure cases.} Figure~\ref{fig:fail1} shows the four worst corrupted inputs, all 35\% occlusions. A cat on a black background is split by a mask that merges with the background (SSIM 0.27); three rectangles hide the head and back of a dog on grass (0.31); an L-shaped mask removes a basset hound's head and the network paints wall and grass instead (0.34); two masks cover a brown dog's body (0.35). The error sits inside the masks and along the outline: the information is absent, so the best the model can give is a smooth guess that matches the surroundings.
